common.zero_machine = Home the machine, then choose the “Machine zero” placement: the design keeps its place on the table, and all steps stay aligned as long as the board does not move.
common.place_stock = Lay the board on the grey “Stock” rectangle (front left corner 10 mm from the origin) and fasten it flat: double-sided tape, or clamps outside the tool path.
common.board_setup = If the board is not already in place, lay it on the grey “Stock” rectangle and fasten it flat.
common.frame = Run Frame: the head traces the outline of the area without cutting. Check that it stays on the board and well clear of the clamps.
common.probe_z = Probe Z on the top of the board (“Probe Z”, in the Run tab).
common.probe_again = Probe Z again: the new end mill does not stick out by the same length, so the old zero is wrong.
common.tool_change = Stop the spindle and wait until it is completely still, then change the end mill without touching the board.
common.start_cnc = Safety glasses on, dust extraction running, hands out of the work area: start the job and stay nearby, ready to press Stop.
common.start_laser = Safety glasses suited to your laser, extraction running or a window open: start the job and never leave the laser unattended.
common.tabs = The parts are held to the board by small tabs: cut them with a utility knife or a fine saw, then sand.
common.spoilboard = Put a spoilboard under your board: through cuts go 0.3 mm into it.
common.cnc_settings = Feed rates are cautious starting values for a small CNC (HiMill type) in plywood; adjust them in the layer settings for hardwood.
common.laser_settings = Powers and speeds are starting values for a diode laser of about 10 W: tune them for your material with the “Laser settings” project.
common.laser_place = Lay the board on the grey “Stock” rectangle (front left corner 10 mm from the origin) and choose the “Machine zero” placement. Without homing: “Head position marks… bottom left”, with the head about 1 cm from the front left corner of the board.
common.laser_board = If the board is not already in place, lay it on the grey “Stock” rectangle and focus on its top surface.
common.focus = Focus the laser on the top of the board.
common.kerf = Cuts compensate for a 0.15 mm kerf: measure yours with the “Laser settings” project and correct “Kerf compensation” in the cut layers if it differs.
common.engrave_first = Engraving runs before cutting, in the same job.
common.hybrid_setup = This project uses the spindle and the laser module of the same machine, one head per step. In Machine › Machine settings…, tick “Laser mode” and fill in “Laser offset” (measured with the “CNC / laser alignment” project).
common.no_move = Do not touch the board again until the end of the project: the laser engraving and the machining must land in the same place.
common.to_laser = Head change: stop the spindle and wait until it is completely still, switch off the power if the module's manual says so, then replace the spindle with the laser module, without touching the board.
common.to_spindle = Head change: switch off the laser, remove the module and refit the spindle, without touching the board.
common.laser_mode_on = Switch the controller to laser mode as the module's manual describes (on GRBL: $32=1).
common.laser_mode_off = Check that the controller is in milling mode ($32=0 on GRBL): in laser mode, the spindle output is cut during rapid moves, which is dangerous for milling.
common.home_again = Home the machine again: with the “Machine zero” placement, the design lands in the same place on the board.
common.laser_focus = Focus: bring the head in Z to the module's focal distance above the board (spacer or mark supplied with the module).
common.offset_check = If the laser engraving and the machining do not line up, the laser offset is wrong: adjust it with the “CNC / laser alignment” project.
common.sheet_back = {0} — back
common.sheet = Layer {0}
common.sheet_front = {0} — front frame
common.stack_glue = Glue the layers in the order of their engraved numbers, from the back to the front (the front frame has no number): a thin bead of wood glue on the frame, not too close to the edges so that it does not squeeze out.
common.stack_pins = Push two toothpicks or 2 mm rods through the alignment holes before clamping: all the layers line up exactly. Trim them flush once the glue has set, or keep the holes to thread a cord through.
common.stack_tint = For depth, stain the back layers darker than the front ones (water-based stain, or strong coffee for the darkest), before gluing.
common.stack_dust = Wipe off the cutting soot with a slightly damp cloth before staining and gluing: it soils the glue and light stains.
common.sand = Sand the edges and faces (180 grit, then 240).

laser_tests.s4.3 = Kerf: push the 10 squares tightly side by side and measure their total length L. Kerf = (100 − L) ÷ 10. Enter it in “Kerf compensation” in your cut layers.
laser_tests.s4.4 = Fit: push the edge of a square into each slot of the comb. The narrowest slot it goes into by hand gives the tight-fit width for this material (useful for finger-joint boxes).

pendulum_clock.s7.2 = Swing the pendulum out about ten degrees and let go. The ticking must be even; if not, turn the anchor slightly on its arbor or shim the clock on one side.
pendulum_clock.s7.3 = If it stops: add weight in 200 g steps, check that nothing rubs, put a drop of light oil or graphite on the pivots (never on the wooden teeth).
pendulum_clock.s7.4 = Regulating: bob lower, the clock runs slow; higher, it runs fast. 1 mm changes the rate by about 1.3 minutes per day.
pendulum_clock.s7.5 = Running time: about 10 hours per metre of weight drop.
