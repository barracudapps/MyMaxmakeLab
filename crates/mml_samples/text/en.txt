laser_tests.s4.3 = Kerf: push the 10 squares tightly side by side and measure their total length L. Kerf = (100 − L) ÷ 10. Enter it in “Kerf compensation” in your cut layers.
laser_tests.s4.4 = Fit: push the edge of a square into each slot of the comb. The narrowest slot it goes into by hand gives the tight-fit width for this material (useful for finger-joint boxes).

pendulum_clock.s3.3 = Slide the drum onto the minute arbor, then glue the great wheel onto the arbor, against the drum. Drive the nail into the hole 31 mm from the centre of the wheel: it is the click pivot. The rubber band, hooked to the second small hole, pushes the click against the ratchet.
pendulum_clock.s3.4 = Glue the three pillars into the back plate, leaving them sticking out 14 mm at the back: they hold the clock away from the wall. Thread the arbors with their spacers, then fit the front plate without gluing it: you will remove it for adjustments.
pendulum_clock.s3.5 = Each arbor must turn freely, with about 0.5 mm of end play. If it rubs, sand the bore or remove a washer.
pendulum_clock.s3.6 = Direction of rotation: seen from the front, the minute arbor turns clockwise, so the escape wheel turns the other way. The tips of the teeth must lead; if not, flip the escape wheel and the anchor.
pendulum_clock.s4.title = Fit the anchor and adjust the escapement
pendulum_clock.s4.1 = Glue the anchor onto its arbor so that both pallets enter equally deep between the teeth when the anchor hangs straight.
pendulum_clock.s4.2 = Turn the escape wheel by hand while rocking the anchor: each tooth must land on a pallet, slide along its sloped face, then escape. The tick and the tock must be even.
pendulum_clock.s4.3 = If a tooth jams, lightly file the sloped face of that pallet, 0.2 mm at a time. If the teeth pass without being held, the pallets have been filed too much: cut a new anchor.
pendulum_clock.s5.title = Motion works, dial and hands
pendulum_clock.s5.1 = Glue the 16 mm pivot into the hole in the front plate to the left of the minute arbor. Glue the minute wheel (30 teeth) against its pinion (10 teeth): the assembly turns freely on the pivot.
pendulum_clock.s5.2 = Press the cannon pinion (10 teeth, module 2.5) onto the minute arbor as a friction fit: this friction is what lets you set the time. If too loose, add a turn of tape around the arbor.
pendulum_clock.s5.3 = Glue the hour wheel (40 teeth) to the end of the 30 mm tube. The tube turns on the minute arbor and the wheel meshes with the minute pinion.
pendulum_clock.s5.4 = Glue the three 6 mm dowels into the front plate, then the dial onto them.
pendulum_clock.s5.5 = Hour hand on the tube (6 mm bore), minute hand on the arbor (4 mm), both at twelve o'clock: a drop of glue for the hour hand; the minute hand stays a friction fit.
pendulum_clock.s6.title = Pendulum and hanging
pendulum_clock.s6.1 = Glue the 10 × 10 mm stick into the notch of the pendulum hanger. Glue the two bobs together and weight them (coins glued in the centre): the heavier the pendulum, the steadier the clock runs.
pendulum_clock.s6.2 = Slide the bob onto the stick, its centre about 560 mm from the anchor arbor. Glue the hanger onto the anchor arbor, behind the back plate.
pendulum_clock.s6.3 = Hang the clock on the wall, perfectly plumb, by the hole at the top of the back plate.
pendulum_clock.s7.title = Starting and regulating
pendulum_clock.s7.1 = Wind the string onto the drum so that the weight turns the minute arbor clockwise seen from the front. To wind up, turn the drum by hand the other way: the click lets it pass.
pendulum_clock.s7.2 = Swing the pendulum out about ten degrees and let go. The ticking must be even; if not, turn the anchor slightly on its arbor or shim the clock on one side.
pendulum_clock.s7.3 = If it stops: add weight in 200 g steps, check that nothing rubs, put a drop of light oil or graphite on the pivots (never on the wooden teeth).
pendulum_clock.s7.4 = Regulating: bob lower, the clock runs slow; higher, it runs fast. 1 mm changes the rate by about 1.3 minutes per day.
pendulum_clock.s7.5 = Running time: about 10 hours per metre of weight drop.
